# halo-record and ISO/IEC 42001

Where halo-record's output sits against ISO/IEC 42001's event-logging control — stated conservatively: this maps *artifacts to a control*, it does not claim conformity for anyone. An AI management system (AIMS) is an organizational program; a record format is evidence inside one.

**Not affiliated with ISO or IEC. Not a conformity assessment.** Control language paraphrased narrowly; the standard itself is the authority. *Last reviewed 2026-09-02, against ISO/IEC 42001:2023 Annex A.*

## The control this speaks to

**Annex A, A.6.2.8 — AI system recording of event logs.** The control's own ask is softer than its reputation: the organisation shall *determine at which phases of the AI system life cycle event log recording is enabled* — a determine-and-document control, not a blanket logging mandate. In practice the pressure arrives from elsewhere: certified organisations get asked by *their customers'* due-diligence questionnaires what their AI systems actually log, and "we determined not to" is a rough answer to write in a DDQ.

## What halo-record produces against it

- **Evidence for the determination.** A.6.2.8 asks you to decide where in the lifecycle event logging is enabled — a documented determination this tool cannot make for you. What a chain of Runtime Records supplies is the evidence behind that document: the records show which phases and boundaries are actually instrumented, and every record's `source` tag discloses how it was captured (LIMITS §3).
- **Evidence with integrity properties.** Where an auditor or customer reviews the logs themselves, hash-chaining means the reviewed artifact is tamper-evident relative to a verified head — a property plain log files do not have, and one the control does not require but reviews reward.
- **Adjacent controls.** Records carry authorization fields, principal identities, and policy-gate verdicts as supplied by the integration — evidence feeding the accountability and oversight controls of an AIMS, with the trust posture stated in LIMITS §10–11 (declared, not externally attested).

## Known gaps, stated plainly

- A.6.2.8 conformity is assessed against your AIMS documentation, not against a tool. halo-record gives you the logging artifact and its integrity story; the determination record, scope statement, and procedures are yours to write.
- No control-by-control mapping of the rest of Annex A is published here. Where other frameworks' logging controls cross-reference ISO 42001 (AIUC-1's published E015 crosswalk cites A.6.2.8), see [AIUC.md](AIUC.md).
